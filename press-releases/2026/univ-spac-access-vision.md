# The vision of universal access to space 

This summer I had the opportunity to meet Candace Johnson, a pioneer in space communication systems. She co-founded and grew SES (Société Européenne des Satellites) into one of the largest satellite fleets in the world. SES is significant because it represents one of the early attempts at privitisation of the space sector along with Intelsat. Prior to this space was exclusively controlled by the government and was confined to mostly research and military operations. 

Led By Candace SES provided Direct To Home satellite and radio broadcast services that democratised information access to millions of citizens in Europe and in the rest of the world. Listening to her speak about how this sector evolved and broke free from the control of the government I was able to connect the dots on how telecommunication became the primary business model of the space industry. 

Historically speaking the evolution of satellites from the initial Low earth orbits to medium earth and geoseationary earth orbits had a tremendous impact on ground communication systems. LEOs required tracking antennas while GEOs improved connectivity by fixed antennas that could continually listen to the broadcast from a fixed point.

Since ground to space communication was such an important part of any satellite deployment it's hardly a surprise that telecommunication became the primary business case of satellites and remains to this day the only revenue generating option. Those initial trials and errors that conceptualised and engineered GEO satellites laid the groundwork for private communication networks to exist. 

The hard work that Candace and other pioneers have put in over the last few decades has borne fruit. The question that people are asking now is what next? Where does the space industry go from here? 

One line of thought is conservative. Telecommunication has brought in billions of dollars in revenue. So what can we do to expand telecomm in space? Sure DTH worked but now people stream movies and sitcoms on their mobile phones. But if there was space internet then perhaps we could capture a part of terrestrial fiber market while possibly offering cheaper services to underserved areas. 

A way to do that is using LEO satellites. Another way , one which Candace advocated ,was bolder but also more robust — deploying base stations on the moon where they could remain firmly grounded and perhaps have an extended life since they don't need to constantly spend fuel maintaining their orbits as satellites. 

There were also several variations of this model like having secure data centers that could offer premium services. 

Then there are the moderates. They believe that while telecom has served the space sector well it is important to expand. While it would be unreasonable to move away from a profitable segment it would be wise to discover new sources of revenue that could sustain the growth in the future. There are some ideas in here that want to push the space industry further. Among them the most noteworthy are wireless energy transfer, space manufacturing ,space tourism and space based planetary imaging systems. 

What's truly lacking though is the same pioneering spirit of early innovators that made the space industry the force that it is today. Privatisation was supposed to democratize space. But has that happened?  I don't think so. 

The space sector has become concentrated in the hands of a few wealthy players who can invest or take loans that enable them to build the infrastructure. This benefits neither the consumers nor the industries. Because the key proposition of the space i.e. cheap connectivity is no longer relevant in the market. Terrestrial fibers have completely outperformed it. There is no way that any space data infrastructure can meaningfully compete with ground based infra. Either in cost or in speed. 

What this has resulted in is bureaucratisation of the sector. Like its initial days the space industry is once more run by governments and militaries. Because it has little economic value left it's declared as a sector that is important for  ‘national security’. Now its chief objectives are surveillance and spying. It's no coincidence that Intelsat is based in Virginia, a place where the CIA is also headquartered and is the single most concentrated location of data centers in the world — with a former CIA agent as its governor.  

What the space industry needs is another revolution that can break down this monopoly of a handful of private corporations working together with the military industrial complex. For that to happen the space industry needs to produce consumer goods. Why can't space materials be used to make durable and eco-friendly cars or create new energy storage techniques?

90% of astrophysics is study of plasmas. Why can't plasma science be applied in chemistry or communication or computing? There is no fundamental reason that it can't be done. The problem is that beaureaucratic space sector has become too dependent on public funding. If astrophysicists can't apply their knowledge for profit in the consumer market they have no one to blame but themselves. 

The intent of space privatisation was indeed noble. But I don't think it has actually delivered on its promise. All it has done is transferred power from the government to the private sector. Space is just as in-accessible as it ever was for the common public and they rarely see any benefits of the research they fund with their taxes. This has to change. 

It was really inspiring to hear Candace’s story and how she became one of the leaders in the industry despite not having a formal background in science. Equally inspiring was her ambitious vision that aims to create a space industry in every nation and hopes that real tangible benefits of the space sector reach ordinary people. Through sheer determination she  has proven that it is indeed possible to make it happen. The question remains: can the next generation of pioneers carry forward her legacy and turn her vision into reality?
