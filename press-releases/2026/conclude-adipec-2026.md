# Concluding remarks on ADIPEC 2026 

Earlier this month ADIPEC in Abu Dhabi, UAE, brought together visionaries in the energy sector for one of the largest conferences in the world. Due to prior commitments I was unable to attend the opening ceremony but followed closely the developments that unfolded during the event. 

While ,despite my best efforts, my schedule often prevents me from engaging directly with the luminaries shaping the energy policy I remain greatly inspired by the remarkable work that they do and feel compelled to offer my own thoughts as a tiny contribution to the scholarly discussions that have already set in motion the energy agenda for the next year. 

Over 2000 exhibiting companies & audiences from around the world gathered at ADIPEC this year. 

With ADNOC as the host this conference was unique because it brought together major oil companies like Exxon,BP,Shell,Petronas ,CNPC and others along with major engineering and tech orgs like Microsoft,Open AI, Siemens,GE,ABB,Mitsubishi and many more represented by ministers ,CEOs and founders. 

This was a very powerful congregation of individuals who deliberated on "turning opportunity in the energy sector into action",thinking in terms of energy addition as opposed to transition and how can capital be mobilised to achieve the objectives of clean and abundant energy.  

It was extremely gratifying to see major oil and clean energy companies setting aside their ideological differences and finding a way to work together on such a big public forum. 

Energy is a matter of national importance. It is what powers the economy. But two forces threaten to disrupt it. 

The first is global geopolitics. Nations around the world have their own interests that are not always aligned with the interests of a particular state. 

An example of this can be seen in the everpresent conflict in the ME itself that always keeps the markets on the edge and prevents nations in ME from realising their full potential. 

This is apparent in the economic disparity that exists between richer Saudi Arabia,UAE,Qatar and lesser developed nations like Iraq, Lebanon,Yemen,Oman,Kuwait. 

Second and more pressing is the rise of the deep state as an organised ,faceless entity  that is Machiavellian in character,follows no ideology and operates along the line dividing corporations from government.

This is a consequence of technological advancement. Politics is no longer what it used to be. 

The days of organising people around a common ideology are all gone. Everyone works for a corporation. That corporation is motivated by profit. Not by national interests and not by public interest. 

Which is why I believe that when leaders designate the energy sector to be of national importance it must recognise that threats are not just geopolitical in nature they are also techno-political and driven by deep state entities over which governments have no control. 

Many of the big national oil companies that are supposed to be state entities are in complex arrangements with private corporations, a result of which is that many high profile government officers have been private employees and many private employees have formerly worked with the government. 

The line separating business and government is not just blurred anymore, it's non-existent.

For the ME as for the rest of the nations in the developing world the resources that they possess can't be economically developed without access to critical technologies that can actualise their potential. The technologies that they don't possess. Leaving only one choice to access them at the cost of sovereignty. Every single state in ME today is militarily occupied by the USA. This is an indisputable fact.  

It was prudent at the time that they made those decisions when economic pressure overpowered other interests but now those decisions need to be re-evaluated. 

The AI revolution that is in full swing right now comprehensively demonstrates how little maneuverability the developing world possesses. 

Setting aside high tech chip manufacturing, complex dependencies exist even for basic electrical and communication infrastructure. 

So while the UAE government was quite enthusiastic in signing a new deal with open AI it remains to be seen if development of data centers would simply be another imported technology or will it actually lead to real skill development?

Another important issue is how to balance new energy generation with established infrastructure that has led to prosperity in the ME? 

Here I fully support the stand taken by organisers who are thinking in terms of addition rather than replacement. 

Oil and gas are dirty only as long as they follow an extractive process but techniques like Fischer Tropsch exist that allow clean hydrogen to be transformed into petrochemicals that can continue to power not just transport but the materials sector esp plastics. 

The problem is that the goals of the oil and gas industry that powers the ME economy are contrary to the goals of RE.

ME would want to capture investment in this sector through profitable projects that are essential to support the economy like desalination but it always comes at the cost of established energy players.

It is possible to break this deadlock by ensuring that RE itself creates new economic value rather than playing a support role. 

Theoretically immense raw solar energy potential exists in ME but again faces the same problem— can that potential be developed? To what extent and with what compromises?

Being a global conference it was natural that questions were asked about the interconnectedness of the world and how national interests need to be balanced with international relations. 

This is where things start to get really complicated. Especially in the energy sector because it's a valuable  and scarce resource. 

Because it's valuable and scarce it has allowed massive oil laundering operations to persist that fund terrorist networks and conflicts around the world. 

The UAE has been involved in wars happening in Africa. ME itself is a conflict zone. Going back a few decades the Bush administration destroyed Iraq under the pretense of WMD. Kuwait has burned in flames of war. Libya is struggling to stabilize itself. These are massive challenges. Without a long term vision it's impossible to tackle them. 

Saudi Arabia is a stabilizing force in the ME. Prince MBS' vision of developing RE,entertainment and agricultural sectors along with massive investment in building new cities has gained international support. But even with all of its wealth SA is facing challenges in funding MBS' vision. 

Of course the situation is complicated by expected palace intrigue and the direct war against Iran by the USA which has resulted in use of attrition tactics. 

Attacks on pipelines and desalination infrastructure are indication of where things could end up if the situation is not controlled. 

Which brings me back to the point I made originally. The deep state has acquired so much power and influence that elected officials and even monarchs are becoming inconsequential. Energy is at the center of this conflict and any strategy that treats it as a national security issue must take that into account. 

President Clinton has made no secret of how the CIA's insubordination prevented his administration from dealing with Al Qaeda's threat leading directly to events of 9/11. 

Subsequent bad intelligence led to the Iraq disaster. Meanwhile, the military industrial complex and powerful lobbyist groups in the Pentagon continue to profit with every war and every regime change.

Unfortunately the US government has been thoroughly infiltrated by organizations like the CIA. Today the USA has governors and senators who are former CIA agents. There is always a cause for optimism but the direction that the US administration is headed in is certainly not one of them. 

With the world in the process of a transition,as new powers in Africa,ME and LATAM are emerging they need to play a greater role in establishing stronger ties with the USA. 

To ensure that its democracy remains intact and it does not descend into an oligarchy. That would be a disaster and it would have an adverse effect on the whole world. 

We must resolve to work together to uproot,dismantle and dethrone the deepstate agents who work in the shadows unelected,unidentified and plot to exercise their control over the rest of the world for nothing but  profit. 

Thank you for your attention.
