# The diplomatic and economic opportunities awaiting Texas under gov Gina Hinojosa

Democratic win in Texas state elections has brought in a change after nearly 30 years of Republican governance. Governor elect Gina Hinojosa has become the first Mexican American who will lead the state of Texas. 

This is significant because of multiple reasons. Texas is the second most prosperous state in the USA and one of the most prosperous regions in the world. 

It is an industrial powerhouse with manufacturing capability spanning space,semiconductors,defense and energy. It's blessed with mineral wealth and new discoveries of rare earth elements has made it a very important link in the global mineral supply chain. 

When Gov. Gina Hinojosa takes the office and she will be deciding the future of a state that has realised its full economic and industrial potential. This presents quite a conundrum for her. 

The state elections were fought on policy disagreements around education and concentration of power in the hands of corporations. Gov Gina has promised to support small businesses and protect and strengthen educational institutions. 

While governor Gregg Abbott lost the elections he is leaving a very robust economy that Gov Gina will administer. His pro business policy helped maintain Texas its lead in the world. 

Though there are challenges with respect to water scarcity and energy distribution they can be easily overcome with the industrial capacity that Texas possesses. 

Some weaknesses remain — for example reliance on imported goods that power the renewable energy stack ,both solar and wind especially permanent magnets but Tx can build those components in house. 

The only thing that could be tricky is managing its energy transition over a course of next few decades if the present data is correct. Texas is a major oil producer and a good portion of its wealth comes from oil and associated products made from that oil. 

The present data seems to suggest that while there are enough oil reserves to meet current production levels for a few more decades its extraction is going to get progressively more expensive. 

Alternative fuels would become relevant at some point as extraction starts hitting its limit. Because oils are also necessary in technical plastics, making hydrocarbons through a green process like Fischer Tropsch could become important. 

But since this is a slow moving problem that will not become an issue in the next several years, the administration might find it difficult to create policies and incentives to accelerate this transition especially with powerful lobbies who have interests in protecting their assets. 

While I support Gov Gina's intent of promoting small businesses the only way they can compete with big players is by doing something novel. 

RE and materials provide that opportunity but it needs to be carefully balanced with the interests of traditional industries. 

Opportunities exist. Neighboring NM would be looking to create jobs for its citizens and it could serve as a market or better an investment destination for Texas to help cultivate the next generation of industries. 

Gov Gina's Mexican heritage could help Texas expand into highly lucrative and rapidly developing Mexico. A recent political shift after nearly a century in British Columbia presents another unique opportunity for Texas businesses to access Canada despite the current highly politicised trade war. 

Texas has been one of the leaders when it comes to producing high quality research. Academic collaborations could serve as stepping stones to something much bigger. 

For Texas there is no real pressure to industrialise or meet its energy requirements. Its fundamentals are quite strong. Energy transition may be turbulent but it can be managed by adept leadership. 

This puts Texas in an incredible position where it can shape the future of technology and industry in developing states(NM) or nations (LATAM and Africa) by leveraging its own advanced industrial base. Instead of depending upon the federal government to create these opportunities Texas needs to take initiative and do what's best for them. This aligns exactly with what Gov Gina Hinojosa intends.
