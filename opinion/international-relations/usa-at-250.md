# America at 250 — The need for institutional transformation 

July 4 2026 marked 250 years of USA’s independence. In 250 years the USA has become the most prosperous nation in the world. It has created new scientific disciplines—electrical,aeronautical,semiconductor and taken existing classical sciences —thermodynamics ,mechanics as well as material engineering to new heights. It has done this while fighting colonialism, famines,civil unrest, the great depression, 2 world wars and numerous other global shocks any one of which would have been enough to end the strongest of empires. 

Regardless of what its critics may say, what the USA has achieved is no small feat. It has accomplished what many considered to be impossible several times in the 250 years since it gained freedom. These achievements are most certainly a cause for celebration. 

The world has changed dramatically in the last few decades and geopolitically new challengers have arrived on the global stage to shape the world according to their ideologies. This changing dynamic was the central theme of discussion at The Meridian Summit last month as thought leaders deliberated on the potency of current American influence and what strategic shifts America will need to make to remain a geopolitical force. [1]


Being a diplomatic forum it was unsurprising that the speakers consistently emphasized the importance of diplomacy and alliances in shaping the world order. 

Historically US diplomats were successful because of their ability to play EU powers against each other. At present US diplomacy works because of its economic and technological dominance. This dominance is not accidental but rather has been a result of century long perseverance and dedication towards science and engineering. A spirit that enabled early American innovators to push through failures with nothing but self belief.

But geopolitics doesn't care about how that technological lead is achieved, only where it resides. This is where I feel that panelists were absolutely correct in identifying that if the technology diffuses or if a new breakthrough happens without the USA it could potentially diminish US geopolitical stature. No matter how much aid the US provides, no matter how generous it becomes with sharing its research and its innovations, it would matter little once competitors catch up and seek to exert their own influence. 

While the US is a modern democracy this dynamic has been true throughout history. What's disconcerting is the fact that the scenario ,about the USA losing its tech dominance, is no longer hypothetical. The pace at which the USA disrupted industries,markets and politics has slowed down. Which has allowed its competitors to catch up. 

Where my views diverged with the speakers at the forum was the role played by the private corporations and institutions in building and retaining American global influence. On the face of it this thought seems quite reasonable. Private corporations in the USA are some of the wealthiest and most advanced in the world and if they engage in direct international partnerships that is only going to solidify USA’s position. 

Well if we look at history this approach was taken by Nixon in China and South East Asia and it has proven to be one of the worst foreign policy disasters of all time. For all the admiration that Kissinger gets for his realpolitik, objectively he is directly responsible for this failure that enabled a communist China to rise. 

Anyway the core reason that private corporations are ineffective in geopolitics comes down to a very simple fact. They have no grand strategy. Their actions are driven by short term profits that fail in anticipating long term consequences. Coupled with the fact that government institutions in the USA have been in decay for the last several decades it's not hard to see why the question of competition has even arised. 


To start with US immigration policy has failed to keep up with the technological diffusion trend. The WW2 era strategy where they thought they could recruit the brightest European minds and have them work for the USA is no longer effective. Now at a baseline technological parity of universal electrification and communication, nations have flooded US universities with deep state agents that are leaking research secrets back home. [2]

Further USA’s economic prosperity has become somewhat of a weakness. Because it has allowed developing nations to provide cheaper labour to manufacture basic goods allowing them to climb up the economic value chain and begin competing with the USA. It has happened at a time when Asian nations have developed rapidly while countries in North and South America lag behind. 

Geopolitically this is as close to a catastrophe as it could have been for the USA and the Americas as a whole. Because now organisations like ASEAN and BRICS are openly thinking about ways of subverting the US led world order replacing it with their own model which they claim to be more just and equitable. [3-5]


The USA has failed on multiple levels in executing this play of technological influence. The government response to this has been predictable. This is what happened in the last few years: CHIPS and science act laid the foundation for reshoring. The Trump administration took that a step further by seeking equity in deep tech companies. Essentially recreating a state led industrialisation model. [6-7]

But the root cause has not been addressed. The problem is that many of the political thinkers seem to naively base their geopolitical analysis on the belief that the state entities are working for the state or the people. This is not true any more. Tulsi Gabbard's expose on federal agencies like CIA and FBI should have been a wake up call but it was largely ignored and even challenged by opponents. However Tulsi is not the only one who is speaking up against state institutions. Present NM governor ,Michelle Lujan Grisham , on several occasions has complained about non cooperation of the FBI to stop drug trafficking in her state but her efforts have been ignored by  the media , federal institutions and think tanks. [8-9]

What has exacerbated the problem is that private corporations are now being run by individuals who have direct political connections with adversaries of the USA. Jensen Huang ,Mark Zuckerberg and Tim Cook have been the most prominent among those but Nadella,Pichai,Banga and others have been quite suspicious in their dealings as well. A clear and direct transfer of knowledge has been established between heads of private corporations and developing nations like India and China. [10]

From a global perspective what's happening in Canada right now should have been alarming for the USA. The unprecedented anti-US sentiment is hardly an encouraging sign. The EU has already declared it does not consider the USA to be a reliable partner anymore and has made public its intention to re-arm itself. Is it surprising that EU and Canada are becoming stronger allies with the EU even offering Canada an associate membership? [11-12]


What's regrettable is that in this confusion USA has forgotten what made it a great country. It certainly was not state control. It was individuals like Edison,or Wright brothers or Shockley who dared to try something new and succeeded. It was private corporations who took on seemingly impossible tasks like laying undersea cable to connect the USA to the EU through telegraph. Today those very individuals and companies find it more rewarding to work for US adversaries. Innovation can't be institutionalized because it's chaotic by its very nature [13-14]. This is why I don't think this state run industrialisation model is going to achieve its goal. It's ok for catching up but not for leading. 


Politically the US made global institutions are weakening. Trust in the UN has been eroded. Secretary Rubio on more than one occasion has treated the International Criminal Court with utmost contempt [15-16]. Not only is the deep state in the USA not cooperating with the institutions it created, it is also being dragged into wars where it has little to gain. Old allies like Israel are becoming a liability preventing the USA from investing in more reliable partners like Saudi Arabia. [17]

What's amusing is that many prominent think tanks continue to give seemingly logical reasons on why the US should continue to carry deadweight when all the evidence points to the contrary. To understand how bad the situation is you only need to look at the prediction markets on the wall street that make people very rich in times of war [18]. Most importantly however, the ideological foundations that made the USA a nation have been replaced by commercial interests giving rise to questions about loyalty and patriotism. Do these terms even hold any meaning? Is the USA a country or a private corporation? 

For a balanced analysis however we need to have a realistic look at what options the USA has available to it. First is the undeniable economic fact that there is only so much growth that you can extract from a particular region. Once that has been exhausted exporting your tech/economic/political model is the most rewarding option that any country could take. 

The problem is that the USA did not effectively strengthen itself before making those choices. If the Monroe doctrine had been followed then LATAM would have been far stronger than what it is today. Had the US foreign policy leaders even the slightest of foresight then post civil rights movement the most reliable partner for America would have been Africa [19]. That opportunity was squandered too. 


While it may sound too pessimistic, the USA still holds some cards. Slight advantages remain in communication and electronics infrastructure. Having the world's largest constellation of satellites and largest concentration of data centers gives it an intelligence advantage. But it matters little when intelligence agencies remain un-trustworthy [20]. Even if they were, it still wouldn't amount to much simply because having intelligence does not mean that you retain the capability to act or deter actions of your adversaries. 

It seems like the US policy makers are operating from an understanding that eventually the US would collapse — its power would diminish. It seems like they have surrendered to this fact which they believe is inevitable. If that is the case then it would be best for them to do what the EU did. Actively participate in creating the next power structures that can continue the domination ideologically if not physically. As mentioned before, the best partner to make this happen is Africa both geographically and culturally since the political transformation that has happened post civil war. 

But I think it would be self-defeating to pursue geopolitics from that perspective. America today needs to answer the question: what does it stand for?Liberty? Democracy? Self Reliance? What is the defining character of America? 

Ultimately, while it's true that the USA is no longer the only one capable of influencing world politics, I also believe it has the right set of universal values that appeals to every human being. What it needs to do now is to rediscover those values and stand by them as Franklin,Washington, Adams ,Madison,Hamilton,Emerson,Douglass ,Lincoln and several other great men in history did.
